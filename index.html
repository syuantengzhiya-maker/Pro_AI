AI and reflection

How I used AI to build this site and my projects.

Responsible AI statement

AI should serve people, not the other way around. I use it as a partner that carries out my ideas, never as a replacement for my own thinking. What to build, who it is for, and how it should work are my decisions. Claude helps me write code faster, find bugs and polish my writing. I read and test what it produces before I use it, and I say openly where I used it.

  • Privacy. The predictor does not store your scores, and this site shares no address, IC, phone number or family details.
  • Honest limits. My model learned from only 91 to 211 students per stream, so I show its accuracy and call the result a suggestion.
  • Checking. I test every piece of code and every number AI gives me before I publish it.
  • Credit. Claude is named as my only AI tool. The ideas, choices and decisions are mine.

AI tools I used

  • Claude (by Anthropic): the only AI tool I used. It helped me write and debug the code for my apps and this website, and polish my English.

Reflection

In one line: AI is my builder's tool. The idea, the decisions and the checking stay with me.

Claude helped me a lot, but the most important part was still my own idea. AI cannot replace the human brain. We should use AI well instead of being controlled by it: think of the idea ourselves, then let AI help us carry it out, rather than letting it think and do everything for us.

For this website, I decided what to show about myself, which projects mattered, and how it should feel. Claude turned those decisions into working code much faster than I could alone. When something looked wrong, I had to explain what I wanted, test it, and ask again. That taught me that a clear idea matters more than a clever tool.

Every time Claude changed something, I checked it carefully. Before I publish anything to Hugging Face or GitHub, I check it again, and if I find a mistake I ask Claude to fix it. Slowly I learned to read code, and now I fix some small errors myself without asking for help.

When AI got it wrong

My app felt laggy, so I asked AI to make it run smoothly. It "fixed" the lag by deleting every 3D effect. The app was fast, but it was flat and no longer looked like mine. I did not accept that. I listed the effects that must stay (the hero depth, the tunnel, the draggable cube, the journey drum) and asked it to fix the real causes of the lag instead.

  • What we changed. The wave background now draws only while the hero is on screen, and the scroll code reads the page layout in one batch before it writes any changes.
  • What I learned. "Make it faster" is too vague. AI will happily remove the part you love. Now I tell AI what must stay, and I test the result before I accept it.
